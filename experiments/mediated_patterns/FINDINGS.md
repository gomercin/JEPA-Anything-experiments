# A mediated field toy with a useful, limited response interface

Exploratory session, 2026-09-19. **Two prepared localized field patterns persist,
interact through a finite-time diffusive mediator, and produce an exterior
response that an ordinary small model predicts across fresh preparations.**
The successful description is a terminal response map. It is not an autonomous
coarse simulator, a demonstrated bound composite, or reusable third-body physics.

![Actual field evolution, causal control, and fresh predictions](../../work/mediated_patterns/analysis-01/mechanism-and-response.png)

The upper-left image is the actual pattern field. The mediator-response heatmap
uses a fixed ±.002 display range; the larger early source response saturates that
color scale. Numeric curves use unsaturated data. Scatter colors indicate nominal
separation, 25 (purple) through 31 (yellow); overlapping points are independent
preparations. No scientific gate depends on plotting.

**Supplied:** SH35 local laws, the mediator source and feedback extension,
periodic geometry, localized initial patches, coefficients, preparation variation,
pulse shape, sensor placement, readouts, and compact regression feature family.

**Generated by evolution:** settled localized configurations; a spatially
extended mediator; a reproducible receiver response with a demonstrable mediator
contribution; persistence of a separated arrangement; an exterior field response
that varies with arrangement and admits accurate small-model prediction.

**Unestablished:** formation from unstructured initial conditions, binding of
the selected pair, physical uniqueness of the description, general microscopic
sufficiency, autonomous reduced dynamics, third-participant reuse, and any neural
or OPF advantage. No neural model was trained. No Atlas or upstream core edit.

## 1. Model selection and qualification

The chosen family is one-dimensional cubic–quintic Swift–Hohenberg, extended by
one finite-rate diffusive mediator. [README.md](README.md) gives equations,
primary-source references, the energy functional, preparation, and commands.
The base localized-pattern regime is documented; the specific mediator extension
is ours, numerically qualified here rather than attributed to the source papers.
The real Ginzburg–Landau front-pinning alternative was inspected but not needed.

The initial feedback neighborhood was g=0/.05/.1, with r=-.67 and b=2 unchanged.
All three produced persistent patches. g=.05 was retained before pair-response
fitting to stay close to the isolated SH35 configuration. It was not selected
for a learned-model result. Source s=1, D=64, tau=10 give mediator length 8.

After 150 units of independent preparation, the single patch persists for another
150 units. Its weighted center changes by less than 1e-15; fractional weighted
mass change is 7.75e-9 with feedback, zero to reported precision without it.
Late whole-field change over 50 units is about 2.2e-12. Weighted mass/width/peak
with feedback are 7.9954 / 2.8889 / 1.3201, versus 7.6349 / 2.8539 / 1.3039
without feedback. The mediator reaches 1% of its settled profile by time 45
(4.5 tau). The pattern both produces and responds to the mediator.

This persistence does not require a pin, registry, bond, or ongoing input.
The equations are overdamped gradient relaxation with homogeneous bistability;
there is no time-dependent drive after preparation except the declared pulse.
The coupled Lyapunov energy decreases in the saved unperturbed pair trajectory
(1.26967466 to 1.26959773). This is not an energy-conserving physical material.

**Numerical qualification.** The retained mesh is L=128, N=512, dt=.0125.
The paired pulse response was compared with dt=.00625, N=1024, and L=256 with
N=1024 at the same coarse spacing. Maximum differences across both pattern
readouts and all saved times are 2.64e-7, 2.93e-7, and 4.30e-7, respectively,
below the unchanged 1e-6 gate. Corresponding exterior mediator discrepancies
are 1.13e-11, 6.84e-11, and 3.85e-8. Boundary images do not explain the effect.
The signal at the primary exterior horizon is about 2.5e-5 RMS.

Two failed qualifications remain visible:

- `qualification-01`: hard-cutoff measurement quadrature changed when grid
  endpoints changed. Remote readout discrepancy reached 6.06e-6 although
  unperturbed fields agreed to 4.08e-8. Replace only the readout with a smooth
  C2 window; retain the failed data and diagnosis.
- `qualification-02`: spatial agreement recovered, but the fast local pulse
  transient still differed by 1.59e-6 at dt=.025. Halve dt again, retaining the
  all-response 1e-6 tolerance. `qualification-03` passes.

No divergence was clipped or omitted. There was no candidate-family switch.

## 2. What interaction is actually mediated?

**Observation.** A localized relative-amplitude pulse at A produces a delayed
change in B. A compact-support pulse prevents direct injection at B. At nominal
separation 28, amplitude +.1 and t=20:

| Pathway | B weighted-mass response | Exterior mediator response |
|---|---:|---:|
| Full coupled evolution | +1.06488e-4 | +2.14331e-5 |
| Sham mediator replay only around B | -2.59277e-5 | +2.05383e-5 |
| Full minus targeted control | +1.32416e-4 | +8.94774e-7 |

**Competing explanations.** Direct SH tails, accidental pulse overlap, global
loss of self-feedback, and mediator feedback at the receiver.

**Tests.** Global unperturbed-mediator replay first showed a pathway difference.
A more selective control then replays only in a fixed B region, keeping A's
feedback and the evolving mediator intact elsewhere. Four saved integrator-stage
values make the control sham agree with full evolution to 4.44e-16. The control
changes neither baseline persistence nor amplitude. The direct u pathway remains.
Alternative readout radii 7/8/9 retain positive full and negative controlled
responses; this is not a particular window's sign artifact.

**Best explanation.** The local `m u` feedback at B contributes positively and
changes the sign of this receiver response. Direct pattern-field influence is
also real and is not attributed to the mediator. B's reaction also changes the
outward mediator signal, by about 4.2% at this horizon. This is a pathway-specific
counterfactual within the toy, not exclusive mediation or causal discovery of
unknown laws. Replay is explicitly open-loop, not a natural alternative world.
A linear mediator can transmit interaction even though its response to prescribed
sources superposes; no nonadditivity criterion was used as a substitute for this test.

## 3. Is the arrangement maintained?

**Observation.** Close initial patches at separations 16/20 reorganized into a
larger patterned region during the early pilot. These outcomes were retained;
they were not counted as survival of two independently tracked components.
The selected separated regime keeps two patches.

At separation 28, after independent 150-unit and joint 100-unit preparation,
both patches survive another 500 units: **50 mediator relaxation times**.
Each weighted center drifts outward only .0362 units, mass changes by about
.0034%, and width is essentially unchanged. The arrangement is weakly interacting
and slowly drifting, **not shown to be a bound composite or equilibrium pair**.
The development/fresh family covers separations 24–32 over the shorter declared
50-unit response horizon; the 500-unit claim is specific to the qualified case.
There are no permanent preparation constraints. Domain doubling leaves the
response and drift effectively unchanged.

## 4. Does organization produce a predictable outward response?

The external input is a specified pulse near A. The literal outward output is
mean mediator in a small sensor centered 12 units beyond B, outside its visible
pattern. The sensor has no dynamics and causes no back-reaction. It is not a
third participant. A second readout measures B's own weighted pattern mass.

An independent-component comparator evolves the isolated A and B references
separately, applies the pulse to A, adds their fields, and subtracts that
condition's own sham. The homogeneous background is zero and is not double
counted. This comparator neglects mutual feedback and direct u interaction.
The isolated references do not receive the joint preparation residual or mutual
preparation history, so this is not an exactly matched-state causal control.
It is deliberately supplemented by a competent interacting response model; the
stage-matched receiver replay, rather than independent-sum error, supports the
mediator attribution.

The compact predictor receives **one measured state descriptor (separation)**
and the known pulse amplitude. Ten equation-informed features combine decaying
mediator and oscillatory SH-tail contributions with linear/quadratic amplitude.
There are 10 coefficients per output horizon, 30 per three-horizon map. These
are ordinary fitted response functions, not learned physical modes. Feature
coefficients are not individually interpreted as causal pathway strengths.

Development uses 10 whole preparations (seeds 11/22; five separations) and three
pulse values, giving 30 responses. Two separate maps predict receiver mass and
exterior mediator. Fits, horizons, readouts and tolerances were frozen before
12 fresh preparations (seeds 101/202/303; four intermediate separations) and
withheld pulses ±.075, giving 24 responses. No fresh case entered fitting.

Held-out errors, pooled over the 24 fresh responses:

| Readout / horizon | Independent NRMSE | Interacting compact NRMSE | Compact maximum absolute error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Exterior m, 5 | 15.59% | 20.62% | 1.73e-8 |
| **Exterior m, 20** | **15.10%** | **1.30%** | **5.30e-7** |
| Exterior m, 50 | 12.30% | 1.56% | 1.77e-7 |
| B mass, 5 | 63.32% | 9.75% | 9.56e-5 |
| **B mass, 20** | **97.86%** | **11.36%** | **5.38e-5** |
| B mass, 50 | 99.67% | 17.13% | 7.88e-6 |

NRMSE divides prediction RMSE by the RMS pulse-minus-sham signal, not by the
large background field. Exterior h20 RMSE is 3.32e-7 versus independent 3.85e-6.
Its per-seed NRMSEs are 1.29%, 1.24%, 1.37%. It passes both frozen gates:
NRMSE ≤15% and maximum error ≤2e-6. At h5 the exterior signal is only 4.18e-8
RMS and the compact model is worse than independent addition; early-arrival
prediction is not a success claim.

**Receiver-map limitation.** B h20 meets the average-error gate but fails its
2e-5 worst-case gate. Errors concentrate at separation 25 with the same sign
across all three preparations; they are not one-seed noise. Refining that case
changes h20 B response by only 1.70e-10 and exterior response by 1.04e-10.
A development-only PCHIP response-gain fit using the same descriptors was tried
on the now-exposed fresh set as a diagnostic. It worsened NRMSE to 22.84% and
maximum error to 1.10e-4. That unsuccessful repair is retained and not promoted
as new confirmation. Sparse response interpolation, nonlinear susceptibility,
and omitted state may contribute; this test does not prove missing information
or the impossibility of a better compact response model. The exterior map's
separate fresh success remains intact.

## 5. Does the description survive microscopic variation?

Fresh preparations differ in isolated seed, shape residuals and joint relaxation
age (20/60). All 12 same-nominal-separation preparation pairs have measured
separation differences below .05 units (maximum .0432) while relative full-field
RMS differences are .33–2.43%. Their positive-pulse B h20 responses differ by
only 2.69e-8–1.04e-6, much less than the failed regression's separation-25 bias.
After diagnostic local centroid alignment, residual field differences remain
.032–.663%; the variation is not solely rigid translation. Alignment is never
used during field evolution or compact prediction.

The exterior model predicts all fresh preparations within the frozen tolerance.
This supports a useful within-family terminal interface across the tested
microvariation. It does not establish that every field with the same separation
has the same response, or that mediator memory can always be omitted. Other
preparation families, strong pulses, mergers, and long-time memory are untested.

## 6. What has actually been compressed?

The main field state contains 1,024 real scalars. At the prediction boundary,
field scans extract centers and discard the fields, retaining separation and
known pulse amplitude. The terminal maps then predict the specified readouts
without future microstate access. For this parameter/preparation/input envelope,
the exterior response is accurately predicted.

Measured illustrative CPU costs: extraction (including unused diagnostic
moments) 88 microseconds; three-horizon response-map evaluation 6.5 microseconds;
a full 20-unit field forecast about .197 seconds. These are local timings,
not a benchmark speedup guarantee. Fitting and preparing fields are excluded
from map-evaluation time and explicitly accounted for separately.

**No recursive closure was tested.** The retained separation is not advanced,
mediator memory is not represented as an evolving state, and there is no claim
that repeated map calls simulate the fields. A concrete missing capability is
a compact state that advances itself and handles the back-reaction of a new
participant without field refresh. That is the next different test if pursued,
not an automatic continuation here. There is no demonstrated residual requiring
neural representation learning: even the remaining receiver error is not yet
separated from ordinary response-function approximation limits.
