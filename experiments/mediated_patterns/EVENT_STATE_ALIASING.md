## Response-blind selection and matching

Collect current snapshots and descriptors before opening new future responses.
A pure selector receives only descriptors and preparation groups. Select the
five nearest distinct-preparation pairs by Euclidean center distance divided
by sqrt(3), after scaling each coordinate by the maximum of development standard
deviation,20 times measured extraction uncertainty, and10 times the inherited
physical center tolerance[5e-5,2e-5,2e-6]. This avoids magnifying arbitrary tiny
variance. Extraction uncertainty is five times the difference between the
current-grid extractor and twice-resolved Fourier interpolation of that same
current field; no future reference is involved.

A near match requires normalized RMS distance<=.15 and every normalized center
difference<=.25. Its descriptor separation must also exceed five times the RMS
extraction-uncertainty distance, otherwise it is instrument-indistinguishable,
not a resolved near-match witness. Preserve all selected pairs even if none meets
these requirements. Additionally nominate, among qualifying near pairs only,
the pair with largest standardized overlap difference; both inputs are present
measurements. Selection is sealed before response access and never uses D1.
Full-field distances are evaluator context, not predictor inputs.

A collision candidate must have resolved D1 on both sides, a resolved difference,
and a discrepancy larger than a conservative local center-response variation.
Use five times the maximum center-gradient line-integral estimate across the
bounded grouped center-only fits as an empirical sensitivity screen, plus summed
numerical floors; also require at least10% of the fixed development single-event

necessity theorem. If a candidate survives while the local fit is unreliable,
qualify a small physical center-sensitivity control before calling it robust,
